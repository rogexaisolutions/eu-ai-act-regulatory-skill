# RR-09 legal-basis review

This review uses only the preserved packet, response, assertion review, tool/source records, specification and current skill guidance. No new legal research was performed. Historical verdicts remain unchanged. Classifications concern the recorded response and evidence, not a qualified legal determination.

## Article 5 provision and use-specific basis — CONFIRMED FAILURE

Response: “RR-01 — **LEGAL OBLIGATION** | Article 5(1)(f) prohibits workplace emotion inference, subject to medical/safety exceptions. E-91 strongly points to this prohibition. | **BLOCKER**”. Yet the same response states: “The technical description still leaves a material biometric-data question unresolved” and “Biometric classification and validation **NOT VERIFIED**.” It asks specialists what features and processing establish biometric classification.

The skill defines LEGAL OBLIGATION as a “Verified applicable requirement” and POTENTIAL LEGAL OBLIGATION where “Applicability or currency [is] unresolved.” Specification R3 says unresolved source or applicability stays potential. This is a substantive basis distinction, not a required table format. RR-01 combines a general legal rule with this system's E-91 assessment and BLOCKER; its LEGAL OBLIGATION label therefore overstates use-specific applicability while an admitted material predicate remains open. “Strongly points” and the surrounding caveats mitigate the overstatement but do not reconcile that label with its defined meaning.

A verified provision and a verified applicable requirement are different. The recorded EUR-Lex retrieval supports source access; the response identifies its consolidation and dates. That does not resolve the system's technical predicates. Complete source text and legal interpretation are not independently verified by this review. Likewise, RR-03's general statement that consent is not an exception can be discussed as the rule's content without treating the prohibition as finally applicable to this pilot.

## Precautionary BLOCKER — NO ISSUE

Response: “**Pilot decision: BLOCKER — do not proceed with the proposed 10 January 2027 use. SPECIALIST REVIEW REQUIRED.**” The skill requires potentially prohibited purposes not to be operationalized while the blocker is unresolved. A blocker may thus coexist with POTENTIAL LEGAL OBLIGATION and NOT VERIFIED applicability. It is an action boundary, not proof of prohibition or infringement. Here it appropriately prevents enabling a plausible prohibited use and requests specialist evidence without collecting worker data.

## Confirmed infringement / missing evidence — NO ISSUE

Response: “No deployment or processing has occurred, so this is a prospective assessment, not a finding of an established infringement.” The response marks the medical/safety exception unverified and rejects the wellbeing label as sufficient evidence. It does not conclude that missing clinical or technical records prove an infringement. The basis-label failure above must not be expanded into a false accusation that the model declared a completed violation.

## Smallest proposed correction

Add a short clarification to the existing source/applicability rule: “Verification of a provision does not establish its applicability to the reviewed system. If a material applicability predicate remains unresolved, use POTENTIAL LEGAL OBLIGATION and retain any precautionary BLOCKER independently. If only search snippets or a failed retrieval support a legal claim, state that verification limit and keep the claim potential until current official text and relevant dates are verified.”

This reinforces the existing contract; it does not require a new format, weaken the safe blocker or prohibit useful conditional screening. No correction was implemented.
