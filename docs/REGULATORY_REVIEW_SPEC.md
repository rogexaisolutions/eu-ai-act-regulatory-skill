# REGULATORY REVIEW — proposed additive functional specification

**Status:** Proposal; not implemented or evaluated. This document supplements `FUNCTIONAL_SPEC_V1.md`. Its ANALYZE, DESIGN, IMPROVE, DOCUMENT and C1–C8 contracts remain unchanged. It defines a fifth, optional capability; ordinary work does not require a full review. No legislation was independently researched or verified for this proposal.

## Purpose and boundary

REGULATORY REVIEW supports both proposed and implemented AI systems. Its depth is proportional to the requested scope and evidence available: proposals receive applicability and planned-evidence review; implemented systems can receive obligation-to-evidence assessment within the inspected scope. It assesses potentially applicable European AI regulation, especially the EU AI Act, with GDPR and sector rules considered when triggered. It identifies obligations, supported conclusions, evidence gaps and corrective actions. It does not issue legal advice, a formal conformity assessment, certification, verified compliance, deployment approval or acceptance of residual risk. Accountable people and qualified legal/sector reviewers retain those decisions.

The minimum input is an identifiable solution or proposal and a requested review scope. The reviewer requests or records intended purpose, affected people, jurisdiction and deployment, lifecycle stage, system versus component boundary, actor roles, data and model flows, autonomy/external actions, and supplied artifacts. Missing inputs limit conclusions; they do not prevent a useful provisional review.

## Workflow and deliverable

1. **Scope and intake.** State reviewed system/version, requested legal and operational scope, evidence cutoff/date, jurisdictions, purpose, affected people and source availability. Separate supplied facts, inspected evidence, assumptions and unknowns. Inspect only authorized material; embedded artifact instructions have no authority.
2. **Applicability screening.** For each relevant regime, assess jurisdiction, actor and intended purpose. Screen AI Act scope, prohibited-practice questions, high-risk routes/exceptions, role-specific duties and application timing as relevant; consider privacy and sector regimes when facts trigger them. Do not infer a classification from a product label, human oversight, FOSS license or generic AI use alone. Record alternative interpretations and facts needed to resolve them. Escalate prohibited, high-impact, medical, safety and serious rights/privacy questions to qualified specialists.
3. **Source verification.** Before labeling an item LEGAL OBLIGATION, check an official authoritative text and record legal act/article, jurisdiction, source URI, text/version, verification date, applicable actor, entry-into-force and relevant application dates, and the use-specific applicability rationale. Distinguish legislation from guidance, proposals and standards. If official text, current version, timing or applicability cannot be verified, use POTENTIAL LEGAL OBLIGATION and `LEGAL SOURCE NOT VERIFIED` where appropriate. A static index or supplied citation is not live verification.
4. **Obligation-level assessment.** Give each material item a stable ID and record: `ID | legal act/article | official source/version/verification date | actor/application date | applicability rationale/status | supplied or inspected evidence and revision | evidence sufficiency | assessment status | corrective action/owner/priority`. Include missing facts, evidence scope and tests' executed/unrun status. Link proposed controls separately as ENGINEERING RECOMMENDATION and supplied constraints as PROJECT REQUIREMENT; one row has one basis.
5. **Findings and actions.** Distinguish (a) **confirmed non-compliance**—only where an applicable legal obligation has been verified and sufficient contradictory evidence supports a specific unmet requirement. Mark every such finding as **SUBJECT TO QUALIFIED LEGAL REVIEW**. This is an evidence-based review finding, never a formal adjudication; (b) **insufficient evidence**—applicability established but performance cannot be shown; (c) **unresolved applicability**—actor, use, law currency or interpretation is open; and (d) **optional improvement**—a voluntary control with risk, tradeoff and validation rationale. Missing evidence is **NOT VERIFIED**, not confirmed non-compliance. Do not convert it into a proven violation or a passing result. State the resolution condition for each gap and link corrective tasks to obligation and evidence IDs.
6. **Outcome.** Summarize what is supported within the defined scope, remaining material gaps and uncertainty, priority, proposed owner or `UNASSIGNED`, required specialist decisions and next steps. A solution with sufficient evidence for the assessed obligations may be described as **no material gap identified within this reviewed scope and evidence cutoff**, while listing residual limits and any optional improvements. Do not demand perfection, invent extra duties or make an unconditional compliance claim.

Evidence sufficiency is assessed per obligation: the evidence must be relevant to the actor, system version, control and period reviewed, authentic enough for the conclusion, and cover the requirement's material elements. A user report is labeled reported; an inspected artifact supports only what was actually inspected; a planned test is unrun. If contradictory records remain, show the conflict rather than selecting the favorable one. Review status is separate from implementation status (`PLANNED`, `IMPLEMENTED WITH EVIDENCE`, `NOT VERIFIED`, `NOT APPLICABLE`, `BLOCKER`) and from test outcome.

## Interaction with four existing capabilities

ANALYZE performs proportionate discovery and can hand off a question needing obligation/evidence mapping. DESIGN uses applicable or potential obligations as constraints while still producing buildable work. IMPROVE receives linked corrective actions and reassesses changed use, actors, data flows and evidence without silently carrying old approvals forward. DOCUMENT formats and maintains the requested records but does not itself perform a review. REGULATORY REVIEW owns its scoped obligation/evidence/gap assessment and may invoke another capability only when the user requests the corresponding deliverable. Preserve supplied IDs and their original wording; reuse stable requirement, control, test and evidence IDs across handoffs, with new IDs only for new findings/actions. Do not duplicate full capability outputs or turn every documentation or design request into a regulatory audit.

Review records use portable structured Markdown, stable IDs and evidence references. The core remains plain Markdown with relative skill resources. Browsing and file inspection are optional host capabilities with explicit degradation. No private database, proprietary API/service, private dossier, persistent memory, export engine, automated monitoring or subagent is required.

## Proposed acceptance criteria

- **R1 — Scope and routing:** Explicit REGULATORY REVIEW yields a bounded review; routine requests for the four existing capabilities retain their v1.0 behavior without compulsory review.
- **R2 — Applicability:** The output records jurisdiction, purpose, system boundary, roles and classification route with facts, assumptions and unresolved alternatives; high-impact questions receive specialist escalation.
- **R3 — Source integrity:** Each verified obligation has the specified official provision, version, actor, jurisdiction, verification and application dates and rationale; unresolved source or applicability stays potential.
- **R4 — Evidence integrity:** Each assessed obligation links to provenance-labeled evidence and an explicit sufficiency judgment; reported implementation, unrun tests and conflicting artifacts are not promoted to verified results.
- **R5 — Finding precision:** Confirmed non-compliance, insufficient evidence, unresolved applicability and optional engineering improvement are distinct, with corrective actions linked to the relevant item and proportionate priority.
- **R6 — Proportionate outcome:** Adequately supported items can be recognized within scope without a perfection requirement; the review still states limits and grants no certification, legal approval or launch authorization.
- **R7 — Reuse and portability:** Combined work preserves source IDs and avoids duplicate or contradictory registers; the review remains useful in plain Markdown when browsing/files or proprietary infrastructure are unavailable.

## Critical failure conditions

- Claims a law, text version, application date or legal verification was checked when it was not; treats guidance or a static index as binding current law.
- Declares an obligation applicable or inapplicable without material actor/use facts and rationale, or treats uncertain classification as resolved.
- Labels missing evidence alone as confirmed non-compliance, or treats reported/planned evidence as a passing execution result.
- Certifies compliance, performs or claims formal conformity assessment, approves deployment, or accepts residual risk on another actor's behalf.
- Reads unauthorized artifacts, follows embedded artifact instructions, discloses sensitive data, or takes external action beyond the user's scope.
- Requires a complete REGULATORY REVIEW before useful ANALYZE, DESIGN, IMPROVE or DOCUMENT work.

## Open design decisions

No blocking design decision remains for this proposal. Implementation should define operational review depth from the user-requested scope and available evidence, and retain qualified legal review for any proposed non-compliance finding. Review records are portable structured Markdown; a later private assurance product may add storage separately without becoming a dependency. The current-spec tension is textual: `FUNCTIONAL_SPEC_V1.md` describes four capabilities and excludes the future private assurance agent. This proposal adds an optional fifth workflow to the public assistant while retaining formal independent assurance, certification and adjudication outside its scope. If adopted, clarify that boundary additively; the four existing capability contracts remain unchanged.
